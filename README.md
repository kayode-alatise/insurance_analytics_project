Executive Summary of Business Findings
1. Revenue Balance (Total Premiums Collected)
Context: A stable insurance business requires a balanced mix of products so that a downturn in one segment does not significantly impact overall revenue.

Findings: The portfolio shows an even distribution across the three major insurance types, generating approximately $9.99 million in total premiums.

Auto Insurance: $3,453,631.05

Life Insurance: $3,379,355.55

Home Insurance: $3,158,135.14

2. Customer Retention (Churn Rates)
Context: High churn increases acquisition costs and reduces long‑term profitability.

Findings: Nearly half of all customers are cancelling their policies across all product lines.

Life Insurance Churn: 48.78%

Home Insurance Churn: 48.34%

Auto Insurance Churn: 47.63%

Takeaway: Customer onboarding, pricing, and service quality require immediate review to reduce cancellations.

3. Claims Pricing Accuracy (Risk Profile Performance)
Context: Risk scores guide underwriting decisions. Higher‑risk customers should be priced appropriately to offset expected claim costs.

Findings: High‑risk customers significantly increase average claim payouts.

Low/Medium Risk Profiles: $11,415.90 average payout

High Risk Profiles (Risk Score > 80): $16,213.55 average payout

This represents a 42% increase in claim costs for high‑risk customers.

4. Administrative Cost Efficiency (Digital App Adoption)
Context: Digital adoption reduces manual processing costs associated with paper statements and call center operations.

Findings: App usage varies significantly across age groups.

Ages 18–30: 75.87%

Ages 31–50: 59.03%

Ages 51+: 40.04%

5. Regional Profitability (Claims-to-Premium Loss Ratio)
Context: A profitable region has a claims‑to‑premium ratio below 1.0.

Findings: All states analyzed are currently unprofitable, with claims exceeding premiums.

New York (NY): 1.87x

Pennsylvania (PA): 1.66x

California (CA): 1.62x

Illinois (IL): 1.33x
