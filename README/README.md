

# Executive Summary of Business Findings

## Overview

The analysis provides an assessment of the insurance portfolio across **revenue performance, customer retention, claims risk, digital adoption, and regional profitability**. The findings reveal a relatively balanced premium portfolio but also highlight significant challenges around **customer churn, high-risk claims costs, digital adoption among older customers, and regional loss ratios**.

Overall, the business generated approximately **$9.99 million in total premiums**, with revenue distributed relatively evenly across Auto, Life, and Home Insurance. However, the high customer churn rates and claims-to-premium ratios indicate opportunities to improve customer retention, pricing strategies, operational efficiency, and regional underwriting performance.

---

## 1. Revenue Balance — Total Premiums Collected

### Business Context

A stable insurance business benefits from a diversified product portfolio. Maintaining a balanced mix of insurance products reduces the risk of significant revenue disruption if one product segment experiences a downturn.

### Key Findings

The portfolio generated approximately **$9.99 million in total premiums**, with revenue relatively evenly distributed across the three major insurance categories:

| Insurance Type     |    Total Premiums |
| ------------------ | ----------------: |
| **Auto Insurance** | **$3,453,631.05** |
| **Life Insurance** | **$3,379,355.55** |
| **Home Insurance** | **$3,158,135.14** |
| **Total**          | **$9,991,121.74** |

### Business Takeaway

The relatively balanced distribution of premium revenue indicates **low concentration risk across the three major product lines**. No single insurance category accounts for a disproportionately large share of total premiums.

---

## 2. Customer Retention — Churn Rates

### Business Context

Customer retention is critical to long-term insurance profitability. High churn can increase customer acquisition costs, reduce customer lifetime value, and negatively affect recurring premium revenue.

### Key Findings

Customer churn is **close to 50% across all three insurance products**, indicating a significant retention challenge:

| Insurance Type     | Churn Rate |
| ------------------ | ---------: |
| **Life Insurance** | **48.78%** |
| **Home Insurance** | **48.34%** |
| **Auto Insurance** | **47.63%** |

### Business Takeaway

The consistently high churn rates suggest that customer cancellations are **not isolated to a single product line**. Management should review factors such as:

* Customer onboarding experience
* Premium pricing and affordability
* Policy renewal processes
* Claims experience
* Customer service quality
* Competitor pricing and offerings

**Reducing churn should be treated as a high-priority business objective**, as even a modest improvement in retention could have a meaningful impact on recurring revenue and customer lifetime value.

---

## 3. Claims Pricing Accuracy — Risk Profile Performance

### Business Context

Risk scores are important for insurance underwriting and pricing. Customers classified as higher risk should generally have premiums that appropriately reflect their expected claims exposure.

### Key Findings

The analysis shows a substantial difference in average claim payouts between lower/medium-risk and high-risk customers:

| Risk Profile              | Average Claim Payout |
| ------------------------- | -------------------: |
| **Low/Medium Risk**       |       **$11,415.90** |
| **High Risk (Score >80)** |       **$16,213.55** |

High-risk customers generate an average claim payout approximately **42% higher** than low/medium-risk customers.

### Business Takeaway

The significant difference in claim costs suggests that **risk segmentation should play an important role in underwriting and pricing decisions**.

The business should evaluate whether premiums for high-risk customers adequately reflect their higher expected claims exposure. Further analysis of claims frequency, policy type, customer demographics, and renewal behavior could help determine whether current pricing adequately compensates for the additional risk.

---

## 4. Administrative Cost Efficiency — Digital App Adoption

### Business Context

Digital adoption can reduce administrative costs by shifting customers from paper-based processes and call-center interactions toward self-service digital channels.

### Key Findings

App adoption varies considerably by customer age:

| Age Group | App Adoption |
| --------- | -----------: |
| **18–30** |   **75.87%** |
| **31–50** |   **59.03%** |
| **51+**   |   **40.04%** |

### Business Takeaway

Younger customers demonstrate substantially higher levels of digital adoption, while adoption among customers aged **51+ is considerably lower**.

This presents an opportunity to improve digital engagement among older customers through:

* Simplified app navigation
* Customer education and onboarding
* Digital assistance and tutorials
* Improved accessibility
* Incentives for using self-service channels

Increasing adoption among older customers could help reduce administrative workload and improve operational efficiency.

---

## 5. Regional Profitability — Claims-to-Premium Loss Ratio

### Business Context

The claims-to-premium ratio is an important indicator of insurance profitability. A ratio **below 1.0x** indicates that premiums exceed claims, while a ratio above 1.0x indicates that claims are exceeding premiums.

### Key Findings

All analyzed states currently have claims exceeding premiums:

| State                 | Claims-to-Premium Ratio |
| --------------------- | ----------------------: |
| **New York (NY)**     |               **1.87x** |
| **Pennsylvania (PA)** |               **1.66x** |
| **California (CA)**   |               **1.62x** |
| **Illinois (IL)**     |               **1.33x** |

### Business Takeaway

**New York represents the most significant profitability concern**, with claims reaching 1.87 times the premiums collected. Pennsylvania and California also show substantial loss exposure, while Illinois has the lowest ratio among the states analyzed but remains above the 1.0x profitability threshold.

Management should investigate regional differences in:

* Claims frequency and severity
* Premium pricing
* Customer risk profiles
* Product mix
* Underwriting policies
* Regional operating and market conditions

---

# Overall Business Recommendations

Based on the findings, the following areas should receive management attention:

### 1. Prioritize Customer Retention

With churn approaching **50% across all product categories**, the business should identify the primary drivers of cancellation and develop targeted retention strategies.

### 2. Review Risk-Based Pricing

The **42% higher average claim payout among high-risk customers** suggests an opportunity to evaluate whether current premiums adequately reflect risk exposure.

### 3. Improve Digital Adoption

The significant adoption gap between younger and older customers presents an opportunity to increase self-service usage and potentially reduce administrative costs.

### 4. Investigate Regional Losses

States with claims-to-premium ratios significantly above 1.0x should undergo detailed profitability and underwriting reviews, with **New York requiring particular attention**.

### 5. Maintain Product Diversification

The relatively balanced distribution of approximately **$9.99 million in premiums** across Auto, Life, and Home Insurance provides a strong foundation for revenue diversification. The focus should now shift toward improving profitability and retention within each segment.

## Conclusion

The analysis indicates that the insurance portfolio has a **strongly diversified revenue base but significant profitability and customer-retention challenges**. While premium revenue is well distributed across products, churn rates near 50% and claims exceeding premiums in every analyzed region create substantial business risks.

The greatest opportunities lie in **reducing customer churn, strengthening risk-based pricing, increasing digital adoption, and improving regional underwriting performance**. Addressing these areas could help the organization improve customer lifetime value, control claims exposure, reduce operating costs, and strengthen overall profitability.
